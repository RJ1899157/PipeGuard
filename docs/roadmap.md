## How the final reviews differ

**Stage 13** overlaps with our recurring audits: it hardens and debugs the integrated system, tests security and failure boundaries, and measures performance after Stage 12 delivery work.

**Stage 14** repeats the broader independent multi-agent audit against the final delivered revision, including changes made during Stage 13. Cover every owned file and documented feature, all buttons and views, contracts, persistence, investigation, recovery, operational commands and documentation. Review unnecessary or duplicated code, but remove it only when behavior and evidence remain correct. Compare representative measurements before and after performance changes.

The final report must identify the reviewed revision, scope, commands and results; preserve earlier failed attempts and recovery history; verify backup restoration and disabled recovery defaults; and distinguish fixed defects, unresolved risks and optional deployment extensions. Recheck fixes through regression tests and running-app verification. Report external dependencies honestly: no audit can establish zero vulnerabilities or universal optimality, and same-host monitoring/backups cannot establish host-loss protection.

## Review gates

- Review database ownership and the logical model before committing to the first schema.
- Validate the pipeline-to-application event contract before broad adapter work.
- Establish evidence provenance before introducing AI hypotheses.
- Review the dashboard design direction before expanding all product screens.
- Evaluate investigation quality and approval controls before connecting recovery execution.
- Complete the integrated demonstration, Stage 13 security/performance review and Stage 14 full-project audit before final delivery sign-off; any production-readiness claim also requires its deployment-specific risks to be addressed.

## Scope controls

Keep initial execution local with synthetic data. Cloud integrations, multi-tenant deployment, extensive distributed processing, and 3D exploration are optional extensions that require their own scope and acceptance criteria. Optimize from measured workloads rather than assuming one design is universally optimal.
